A community thread usually starts with a scenario, not a feature list. Someone is trying to complete a specific thing in Dynamics 365. The product offers more than one path. The useful answer is which path fits, and what it breaks when the data or the security role changes.

That is what these blogs are for.

I take part in those discussions. A blog is the longer form of a good answer: the scenario, the functionality involved, the options, and the one I would choose. Dataverse, model-driven apps, Power Automate, security, and solution ALM will show up because that is where the questions are.

## What a post will cover

- The scenario, stated the way it shows up in a community question
- The Dynamics functionality that actually decides it
- The options, including the one that looks right and is not
- What to verify before you rely on it

I am writing for people who configure and extend this stack. I will not restate the product documentation.

## A bar for the next post

A scenario is specific enough when a reader can tell whether it is their case.

```text
scenario:  what the person is trying to complete
feature:   the table, form, flow, or security behavior involved
options:   the path that looks obvious, and the one that holds up
check:     what still fails when the data or the role changes
```

If a post cannot name the scenario and the option turned down, it is not ready.
